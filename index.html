About Grey Book Reflections

Review & Input, Open Participation, Home-Group Autonomy & Direct Responsibility

Grey Book Reflections is Review & Input literature drawn from the personal experiences of recovering addicts within Narcotics Anonymous. Its reflections are based on lines found in the Memphis 1981 Review & Input form of the Basic Text, also known as the Grey Book.

According to its Preface, the project began in May 2013 when a Group in Allentown, Pennsylvania wished for a daily reflection book written by addicts for addicts through an open participation process.

In 2014, that Group together with other Groups sponsored a literature-writing conference in Longwood, Florida to begin the writing process.

Members attended from Kentucky, Ohio, Florida, Georgia, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, North Carolina, and South Carolina. The Preface states that some of these members had also participated in writing the Basic Text.

Additional writing continued at literature conferences and in NA Home Groups around the world through what the Preface describes as an open participatory literature process. That input was assembled into the book called Grey Book Reflections.

NA Home Groups and members are encouraged to become involved, participate, and submit input. The Preface closes in service from “The N.A. Home Groups and Fellowship Service Conference.”

What this site is

The homepage of the Grey Area Group of Narcotics Anonymous, featuring the Daily Grey Book Reading, historical literature study meetings, A.S.I.S. for NA and other historical literature, announcements, audio, service information, and additional Group resources.

The Grey Area Group is autonomous. This is not the official website of A.S.I.S., NAHELP, the Fellowship Service Conference, NA World Services, or the entire Fellowship of Narcotics Anonymous.

A.S.I.S. for NA describes itself as

“A world-wide NA Home Group designed to provide our trusted servants—whose service has sometimes isolated them from their local groups—with the love and understanding they need to survive.”

The Grey Area Group shares literature and service information connected with A.S.I.S. while retaining its own Group conscience, autonomy, and direct responsibility for this website.

Grey Book Reflections is separately presented as Review & Input literature developed through an open participatory process involving NA Home Groups and literature conferences, with the Fellowship Service Conference identified in its Preface.

Principles behind this work

The material below summarizes service and participation principles represented in the historical and A.S.I.S. material available through this site. Open any heading to read more.

True Trusted Service

Trusted servants are selected to perform work the Groups need. Their role is service rather than government: they stay informed, report back honestly, seek guidance, and remain accountable to the members and Groups whose trust they carry.

Directly Responsible

Direct responsibility works in both directions. Service bodies need clear instructions from the Groups, and Groups need complete and accurate information from their trusted servants. Authority begins with the Group conscience and flows outward only for the work the Groups have requested.

Autonomy Without Isolation

Group autonomy gives each NA Home Group the freedom to shape its meeting, literature, and service participation within the Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions. Autonomy is not separation from the Fellowship; it allows Groups to cooperate freely without being governed by a service body.

Why A.S.I.S.?

A.S.I.S. grew from concern that trusted servants and autonomous Groups could become isolated when questioning established service practices. Its stated purpose is to offer connection, historical information, literature, and a home-group setting where service can be examined against the Traditions.

Open Participation & Literature

Grey Book Reflections describes its own development as an open participatory literature process involving NA Home Groups, literature conferences, and members from different parts of the Fellowship.

Draft, Review & Input, home-group-approved, historically approved, and broadly approved literature are labeled separately on this site so readers can understand each document’s standing.

A New Surrender

The service inventory described in the NAHELP material asks members to step back from habit, personalities, and structural conformity, then compare service practices with unity, Group conscience, self-support, and direct responsibility.

The goal is not control, but a return to principled service that helps recovery become available.

Autonomy & Service Documents

These documents present a home-group-autonomy approach to NA service and literature. Both are drafts and are provided for review, discussion, editing, and Group conscience—not as adopted policy of the Grey Area Group or any other NA Group unless that Group formally approves them.
